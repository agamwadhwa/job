# Approvals & liaison

Covers UDCPR 2020 (Maharashtra outside Mumbai, including PMC, PCMC and PMRDA), DCPR 2034 (Mumbai/MCGM), CC/OC and RERA. Rates, thresholds and clause numbers change through amendments and circulars. Everything numeric here is **verify current**, and the interview-safe phrasing is "under UDCPR as I understand it, subject to the latest amendment".

## Primer: concepts before mechanics

**Why approvals are a money question.** Every approval changes either how much you can build (FSI), when you can sell (RERA registration needs approved plans), or when you can hand over (OC). A 6-month delay on REF-W drops unlevered IRR from 23.6% to about 21.6%, before any cost inflation. Liaison isn't paperwork. It's the schedule on which the feasibility's cash flows depend.

**Who approves what.**
- **Pune:** PMC (Pune Municipal Corporation) inside city limits, including the villages merged in 2017 and 2021 (Wagholi was among those merged in 2021: **verify**). PCMC for Pimpri-Chinchwad. PMRDA for the metropolitan region outside the corporations. All use **UDCPR 2020**, the Unified Development Control and Promotion Regulations that replaced separate DCRs across Maharashtra except Mumbai and a few special areas.
- **Mumbai:** MCGM under **DCPR 2034**. Parallel authorities handle specific schemes: SRA (slums, 33(10)), MHADA (its colonies, 33(5)), the Repair Board/MBRRB (cessed buildings, 33(7)), and MMRDA and CIDCO in their notified areas.

**FSI building blocks under UDCPR (residential, typical):**
- **Basic FSI:** the base entitlement, 1.1 in many municipal areas (**verify for the specific authority**).
- **Premium FSI:** additional FSI bought from the authority at a % of the ASR land rate. Commonly 0.5 (**verify**).
- **TDR:** Transferable Development Rights, bought in the market from people who surrendered land (road, reservation) elsewhere. The cap depends on **road width**. A commonly cited UDCPR pattern for municipal corporations is roughly: 9–12 m road → 0.4 TDR (in some bands); 12–15 m → 0.65; 15–24 m → 0.9; 24–30 m → 1.15; ≥ 30 m → 1.4 (**verify the current table**; REF-W uses 0.9 for an 18 m road).
- **Ancillary FSI:** up to 60% of the FSI above (for residential), paid at a % of ASR. It covers staircases, lifts, lobbies and similar areas, which are otherwise charged (**verify rate**; REF-W assumes 10% of ASR).
- **In-situ FSI / TDR for surrendered land:** land given up for road widening or reservations can earn FSI on the remaining plot or TDR (often at a multiple: **verify**).

**FSI building blocks under DCPR 2034 (Mumbai):** zonal (basic) FSI + additional FSI on premium + TDR (road-width linked) + **fungible compensatory area** (up to 35% for residential on premium: **verify**). Then scheme-specific incentives: 33(7)(B) for old societies, 33(9) for clusters, 33(10) for slums, 33(11) for PTC/PAP housing, 33(12)(B) for old buildings on specified lands (**verify**).

**Approval sequence (typical, both cities):**
1. **Pre-approval inputs:** title and zone, DP remarks, measurement, tree survey, and NOCs as needed (fire for high-rise, airport height (AAI/NOCAS), heritage, CRZ near coasts and creeks, environment clearance above a built-up threshold (currently 20,000 sq m under the EIA notification: **verify**)).
2. **Building permission / plan sanction.** In Mumbai, the first stage is the **IOD** (Intimation of Disapproval), which is approval with conditions.
3. **Commencement Certificate (CC).** Often issued in stages (up to plinth, then full height).
4. **RERA registration:** needs approved plans, CC, title certificate, and the project's financial and completion schedule. No advertising or sale before registration.
5. Construction, with stage inspections, fire NOC stages, and plinth checking.
6. **Occupancy Certificate (OC)** (Pune uses "completion/occupancy"). Only then is possession lawful. RERA requires possession by the declared date or interest to buyers.

**RERA essentials:**
- **70% rule:** 70% of amounts collected from allottees are deposited in a separate project account and used only for that project's land and construction costs. Withdrawals are in proportion to completion, certified by an engineer, architect and CA (Maharashtra's Form 1/2/3 mechanics: **verify current**).
- **Carpet area:** the net usable floor area of a flat, excluding external walls, services shafts, exclusive balcony/verandah and open terrace, but including internal partition walls. Sales must be quoted in carpet.
- **Quarterly progress reports (QPRs)** on the MahaRERA portal show bookings and construction status. This is the best free source of competitor absorption data.
- **Defect liability:** 5 years for structural defects after handover.

## 15 interview questions with model answers

### Q1 [appr] What changed when UDCPR 2020 came in?
Before December 2020, each Maharashtra authority had its own development control rules, so a feasibility in PMC and one in PCMC used different FSI, margins and premium rules. UDCPR unified these for the state outside Mumbai and a few special areas. For developers, the main effects were more FSI in many areas (basic + premium + road-width-linked TDR + ancillary), a standard structure that made feasibilities comparable, and ancillary FSI replacing a patchwork of free and chargeable areas. Pune's merged villages also moved onto a consistent regime. Practically: FSI potential on wider roads rose sharply, and TDR demand and prices rose with it. I'd add that UDCPR has been amended several times, so I always check the current consolidated version before relying on any number.

### Q2 [appr] Explain the difference between premium FSI, TDR and ancillary FSI.
All three add buildable area beyond basic FSI, but they're bought differently. Premium FSI is bought from the planning authority at a rate linked to the ASR land rate, so it's a fixed, predictable cost. TDR is bought in the open market from holders of development rights generated elsewhere (for example from land surrendered for roads). Its price moves with supply and demand, and how much you can load depends on road width. Ancillary FSI is an add-on of up to 60% of the permissible FSI for residential, charged at a % of ASR. It accounts for common areas like staircases and lobbies. In a feasibility: premium and ancillary are formula costs, and TDR is a market-risk cost. That's why TDR price is one of the sensitivities I'd always run.

### Q3 [appr] Why does road width matter so much for a Pune feasibility?
Because the TDR cap, and so the total FSI, is tied to the width of the road the plot abuts. On REF-W, an 18 m road allows 0.9 TDR. If the same 2 acres fronted a 12 m road, the TDR cap would be lower (say 0.65: **verify the table**), and FSI area would fall by roughly 10%. Road width also governs building height and fire access. A proposed DP road can help (future width may count once it's acquired, subject to the rules) or hurt (you lose land to road widening, though usually compensated by FSI or TDR). So road width is one of the first things I check: from the DP remarks, from measurement on site, and whether the road is existing and physically open, not just proposed on the plan.

### Q4 [appr] Walk me through the approvals for a 22-storey residential tower in Pune.
Start with inputs: title and zone confirmation, measurement, tree survey, and any required NOCs: fire (high-rise), airport height clearance if within the funnel, environment clearance if built-up area crosses the EIA threshold (**verify current threshold**). Then apply for building permission to PMC/PCMC/PMRDA with plans, the area statement, and payment of scrutiny fees, premium and development charges. The authority sanctions the plan and issues a commencement certificate, sometimes in stages. With the plan and CC, register on MahaRERA before any marketing. During construction: plinth checking and fire NOC stages. At completion: fire final NOC, lift licences, structural stability certificate, and then the OC. The critical path is usually environment clearance and fire for a tower of that height.

### Q5 [appr] What's an IOD in Mumbai and how is it different from a CC?
The IOD (Intimation of Disapproval) is MCGM's first approval of the building plans. Despite the name, it's an approval subject to a list of conditions to be complied with before or during construction. It fixes the plan, FSI and premiums. The Commencement Certificate (CC) actually permits construction to begin, usually first up to plinth, then extended after plinth checking. Many IOD conditions are staged: some must be met before CC, some before OC. For a feasibility, the IOD date locks the regulatory basis. The CC date is when construction and RERA-based selling can start. The gap between them depends on how fast premiums are paid and conditions met.

### Q6 [appr] What's the 70% RERA rule, and when does it bite?
Under RERA, 70% of money collected from buyers must go into a separate project account and can only be used for that project's land and construction costs. Withdrawals are certified in proportion to completion. The idea is to stop developers funding new land with buyers' money. Whether it bites depends on margin. In REF-W, 70% of total revenue (₹136 cr) is less than land-plus-construction cost (about ₹144 cr), so it never traps cash beyond cost. In a high-margin Mumbai project it does: if revenue is ₹1,000 cr and land and construction are ₹450 cr, 70% of collections (₹700 cr) exceeds cost by ₹250 cr, and that surplus is locked until completion. That's why high-margin developers care about it and lenders like it.

### Q7 [appr] What is RERA carpet area and how does it relate to FSI area?
RERA carpet area is the net usable floor area of the apartment: everything inside the external walls, including internal partitions, excluding services shafts, exclusive balconies/verandahs and open terraces. FSI area is what counts against the permissible FSI under the DCR, and it includes things RERA carpet excludes: walls, parts of common areas depending on the rules, and so on. So carpet is always less than FSI area. REF-W assumes carpet at 78% of FSI area including ancillary. The real ratio comes from the architect's area statement, and it varies with design: floor plate efficiency, balcony treatment, and how much common area counts against FSI. A 5-point error in this ratio moves revenue by about 6%. That's why I'd challenge any feasibility that just assumes it.

### Q8 [appr] What's 33(7)(B) of DCPR 2034?
It's the provision for redeveloping existing residential housing societies that aren't cessed buildings (covered by 33(7)) or MHADA buildings (33(5)). For societies older than 30 years, it allows incentive built-up area of 15% of existing BUA or 10 sq m per tenement, whichever is more, without premium, when existing members are re-accommodated on the same plot. If existing BUA plus incentive is still below the permissible FSI, the balance can be added using premium FSI or TDR up to the permissible limit ([summary](https://kutirgroup.in/blog/2025/09/24/regulation-337b/)). **Verify current text.** Commercially, it's the mechanism that funds larger flats for members plus free-sale area for the developer in suburban Mumbai.

### Q9 [appr] What's the difference between 33(7), 33(9), 33(10) and 33(11)?
They're DCPR 2034's scheme-specific redevelopment routes. 33(7) covers cessed buildings in the island city: old tenanted buildings repaired by MBRRB, with incentive FSI for rehabilitating tenants. 33(9) is cluster redevelopment, or urban renewal: large contiguous areas redeveloped together with higher FSI, subject to minimum area and consent conditions. 33(10) is slum rehabilitation through the SRA: free rehab tenements for eligible slum dwellers, funded by a sale component, with the ratio linked to land rates. 33(11) covers PTC/PAP tenements for project-affected people (**verify**). In an interview I'd name them correctly, explain the economic logic of each, and say I'd check the latest amendments for the exact ratios.

### Q10 [appr] How does a liaison consultant add value, and what should the developer never outsource?
A good liaison consultant knows the authority's process, the documents each desk expects, the order of NOCs, and how to respond to scrutiny remarks quickly. That can cut months. They prepare files, track the approval status, and coordinate premium payments. What the developer must own: the regulatory interpretation that drives the feasibility (for example, whether a road counts as 18 m or 15 m, or whether an incentive applies), the decision to pay premium vs load TDR, and compliance. The legal risk of a wrong approval sits with the developer and, through RERA, with buyers. So I'd want our own team to read the regulation and challenge the consultant's reading, not just receive status updates.

### Q11 [appr] What happens if a project gets its OC late?
Possession can't lawfully be given without an OC. Buyers are entitled under RERA to interest for delay beyond the declared date (or refund with interest if they withdraw). Utilities and society formation also depend on the OC. Commercially: the final instalments (often 5–10% on possession) are held up, finance costs run on, and the brand takes damage. Common causes: fire final NOC delays, deviations from sanctioned plans that need regularisation, incomplete conditions from the IOD/CC, and pending premium dues. The prevention is a condition tracker from day one: every condition, its stage, owner and status, reviewed monthly.

### Q12 [appr] How do you decide between premium FSI and TDR when both are available?
Compare cost per sq ft of sellable area and timing. Premium FSI is paid to the authority at a set rate, which in REF-W works out to about ₹67 per sq ft of carpet. TDR is bought in the market at ₹2,200/sq ft (assumption), which is about ₹635 per sq ft of carpet. Both are usually needed to max out the plot, since each has its own cap. The real decision is whether to load TDR at all. If the marginal carpet from TDR (plus its ancillary) sells for well above TDR cost plus construction cost, load it. Timing matters too: TDR prices move, so buying early locks the cost but ties up capital. If the TDR market is tight, securing supply may be the constraint, not price.

### Q13 [appr] What are the key NOCs for a high-rise in Mumbai?
Fire (the CFO's NOC at plan stage and final), which becomes critical above certain heights. Height clearance from AAI through the NOCAS portal, especially near Juhu and the Santacruz/Sahar airport funnel. Environment clearance if built-up area crosses the threshold. The Tree Authority for felling or transplanting. MCZMA for CRZ areas near the coast and creeks. Heritage committee for heritage precincts. Railway or metro NOCs near those corridors. And traffic and parking approvals for large projects. The set depends on location, so I'd pull the DP remarks and a site-specific checklist first. Height clearance near the airport can cap the building below what FSI allows. That makes it a feasibility constraint, not just a formality.

### Q14 [appr] What's in a MahaRERA registration and what does it commit the developer to?
The developer uploads the approved plans and CC, title certificate, land cost, estimated cost, the proposed completion date, the carpet area and number of units, details of the promoter, architect, engineer and CA, and the designated bank account. Once registered, the developer must market and sell only as registered, deposit 70% of collections in the project account, file quarterly progress reports, deliver by the declared date or pay interest, and keep the plans unchanged without allottees' consent (two-thirds for certain changes: **verify**). It's a public commitment on timeline and specification. For a feasibility, the declared possession date should include a realistic buffer, because breaching it is expensive.

### Q15 [appr] What's the ASR (ready reckoner) rate and why does a liaison person care?
The Annual Statement of Rates is the government's published minimum value for land and property by zone, used for stamp duty. Many approval charges are linked to it: premium FSI, ancillary FSI, some development charges and conversion charges are computed as a % of ASR land rates. So when ASR rises, approval costs rise. Maharashtra kept ASR rates unchanged for FY 2026-27 after increases in FY 2025-26 (PMC areas about 4.2%, PCMC about 6.7%, Mumbai about 3.4%) ([newkerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/maha-govt-maintains-status-quo-ready-reckoner-rates-682.htm); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/maharashtra-hikes-property-rates-what-3-9-rr-rate-increase-means-for-you-125040100372_1.html)). I'd check the current year's rates in every feasibility.

## 5 numeric problems

### P1 [appr] Premium FSI cost on REF-W
**Given (assumptions):** net plot 7,700 sq m. Premium FSI 0.5. Premium charged at 50% of the ASR open-land rate. ASR = ₹9,000/sq m. **Verify** the current premium % and ASR for the zone.

**Solution:**
- Premium FSI area = 0.5 × 7,700 = 3,850 sq m
- Rate = 50% × ₹9,000 = ₹4,500/sq m
- Cost = 3,850 × 4,500 = **₹1.73 cr**
- Per sq ft of total carpet: ₹1.73 cr ÷ 2,58,592 ≈ **₹67/sq ft**

**Point to make:** premium FSI is cheap relative to TDR. That's why it's almost always taken in full.

### P2 [appr] Ancillary FSI cost
**Given:** ancillary = 60% of (basic + premium + TDR) FSI area = 60% × 19,250 = 11,550 sq m. Charged at 10% of ASR (₹9,000/sq m). **Verify the rate.**

**Solution:**
- Cost = 11,550 × 0.10 × 9,000 = **₹1.04 cr**
- Ancillary area = 11,550 × 10.764 = 1,24,323 sq ft → **₹84 per sq ft** of ancillary area.

**Point to make:** ancillary is what makes a UDCPR building efficient. Without it, common areas eat into the FSI area available for flats.

### P3 [appr] Cost of a 6-month approval delay
**Given:** REF-W, with land paid at quarter 0 and every other cash flow shifted 2 quarters later. Cost of capital 14%.

**Solution:**
- Base unlevered IRR = 23.6%. Delayed = **21.6%** (from the REF-W script).
- Carry on land + stamp + early approvals (₹19.1 cr) for 6 months at 14% ≈ 19.1 × (1.14^0.5 − 1) ≈ **₹1.3 cr**.
- Unmodelled (and often larger): construction cost inflation over 6 months (at 5–6% a year, about 2.5–3% of ₹99 cr ≈ ₹2.5–3 cr) and a possible drop in launch demand if competitors launch first.

**Point to make:** the IRR hit from the delay alone looks modest. The real cost is carry plus inflation plus market timing. A good answer names all three.

### P4 [appr] RERA 70% account: does it trap cash?
**Given (assumptions):** a Mumbai luxury project. Revenue ₹1,000 cr. Land (including premiums and approvals) + construction cost = ₹450 cr. By completion, collections are 100% of revenue.

**Solution:**
- Deposited in the RERA account = 70% × 1,000 = ₹700 cr
- Withdrawable for land + construction (certified) = ₹450 cr
- **Trapped until completion/OC = ₹250 cr.** (Released once the project completes, subject to the rules: **verify current**.)
- Compare REF-W: 70% × ₹194 cr = ₹136 cr < land + construction ≈ ₹144 cr (land 16 + stamp 1.1 + premiums/TDR/statutory 24.1 + construction and fees 103.1 = 144.4). **Nothing trapped.**

**Point to make:** the 70% rule mainly constrains high-margin projects, where it stops profits funding the next land deal.

### P5 [appr] Fungible FSI in Mumbai
**Given (assumptions):** a 2,000 sq m plot in Andheri West. Permissible FSI (zonal + premium + TDR on this road) = 2.5 (**verify DCPR Table 12 for the road width**). Fungible compensatory area 35% on premium at 50% of ASR (**verify rate**). ASR land rate ₹1,50,000/sq m (assumption). Sale rate ₹32,000/sq ft carpet. Carpet = 90% of fungible-inclusive FSI area for this component (assumption).

**Solution:**
- Base FSI area = 2,000 × 2.5 = 5,000 sq m
- Fungible area = 35% × 5,000 = 1,750 sq m = 18,837 sq ft
- Fungible premium = 1,750 × 50% × 1,50,000 = **₹13.1 cr**
- Carpet from fungible ≈ 18,837 × 0.9 = 16,953 sq ft → revenue ≈ 16,953 × 32,000 = **₹54.3 cr**
- Construction on fungible area at ₹5,000/sq ft of construction area (with a 1.4× gross-up) ≈ 18,837 × 1.4 × 5,000 = ₹13.2 cr
- Incremental surplus ≈ 54.3 − 13.1 − 13.2 = **₹28.0 cr**. Take the fungible area.

**Point to make:** on high sale-rate Mumbai land, fungible premium is almost always worth paying. The question is whether the design can use it (floor plate, height clearance).

## Common traps and wrong answers
- **Quoting a single "Pune FSI".** FSI depends on authority, zone, road width and scheme. Always give the structure.
- **Treating TDR as a fixed cost.** It's a market price and a key sensitivity.
- **Mixing up IOD and OC**, or saying "OC is issued before possession is optional". Possession without an OC isn't lawful.
- **Saying RERA "locks all profit"**. It traps surplus only where 70% of collections exceed land plus construction cost.
- **Forgetting height clearance near airports**, which can cap FSI use in Andheri, Vile Parle and Santacruz.
- **Ignoring environment clearance** on large projects. It's often the critical path.
- **Citing clause numbers or percentages confidently without "verify current".** Amendments are frequent. Precise but wrong is worse than approximate with a caveat.
