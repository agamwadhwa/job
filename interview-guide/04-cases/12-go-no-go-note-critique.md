# Case 12: a Go/No-Go note critique

## Prompt
A junior colleague has written this note for the promoter. Critique it: what's wrong, what's missing, and what would you change? (Find at least eight issues.)

> **GO/NO-GO: 2 acres, Wagholi. Recommendation: GO**
>
> Land ₹16 cr. FSI 2.5 on 2 acres = 5 acres of construction = 2,17,800 sq ft. Super built-up at 1.3× = 2,83,140 sq ft. Price ₹5,800/sq ft super built-up → revenue ₹164 cr.
>
> Construction ₹2,300/sq ft × 2,17,800 = ₹50 cr. Land ₹16 cr. Approvals ₹3 cr. Marketing ₹5 cr. **Total ₹74 cr. Profit ₹90 cr (55%!).**
>
> Market is strong; neighbour sold out in 6 months. Landowner will sign this week. Recommend GO.

## 5-minute framework answer
Walk through the errors in order of impact, then rebuild the numbers:
1. FSI applied to **gross plot** and **acres** ("5 acres of construction"). There are no deductions and no ancillary.
2. **Super built-up** used for sales. Pricing must be RERA carpet.
3. **No TDR cost**, yet FSI 2.5 includes 0.9 TDR (≈ ₹16 cr).
4. **Construction cost applied to FSI area**, not construction area (parking, services). Understated by about half.
5. **Missing costs:** premium FSI, ancillary, stamp duty, statutory charges, professional fees, admin, finance.
6. **No timing:** no IRR, no peak funding, no absorption assumption.
7. **Evidence:** "neighbour sold out in 6 months" is one data point, and it's untested.
8. **No risks or diligence:** title, DP remarks, road width, approvals timeline.
9. **Pressure framing:** "landowner will sign this week" isn't a reason.
Rebuilt on REF-W assumptions: **16% margin, 23.6% IRR, ₹55 cr peak funding.** That's a *conditional* go, not a 55% slam dunk.

## Full numbers: error by error

| Line in note | Problem | Corrected (REF-W basis) |
|---|---|---|
| "FSI 2.5 on 2 acres = 5 acres = 2,17,800 sq ft" | Uses gross area. Ignores road-widening deduction and 60% ancillary | Net 7,700 sq m × 2.5 × 1.6 = **3,31,528 sq ft FSI area** |
| "Super built-up at 1.3× → ₹5,800/sq ft SBU" | RERA requires carpet pricing. SBU inflates area | Carpet 78% = **2,58,592 sq ft** × ₹7,500 = **₹193.9 cr** (₹7,500 carpet ≈ ₹5,800 on a 1.3× SBU basis, so the price itself is similar) |
| "Construction ₹2,300 × 2,17,800 = ₹50 cr" | Wrong area (should be construction area = 1.3× FSI area) and too small a base | 4,30,987 × ₹2,300 = **₹99.1 cr** (+ 4% fees = ₹103.1 cr) |
| No TDR | FSI 2.5 includes 0.9 TDR | 74,594 sq ft × ₹2,200 = **₹16.4 cr** |
| "Approvals ₹3 cr" | Missing premium, ancillary, statutory, stamp | Premium 1.7 + ancillary 1.0 + statutory 5.0 + stamp 1.1 = **₹8.8 cr** |
| "Marketing ₹5 cr" | Low, and no admin | Marketing 5% = 9.7, admin 2% = 3.9 |
| No finance | | ₹5 cr |
| **"Profit ₹90 cr (55%)"** | | **Profit ₹31.0 cr (16.0%)**, IRR 23.6%, peak ₹54.7 cr |

**What the corrected note says:** below a typical 18–20% margin hurdle, but an acceptable IRR. **Conditional GO** if: (a) land price moves to ₹14 cr, or the deal goes JDA at ≤ 15% revenue share; (b) absorption is supported by a market-share analysis, not one neighbour; (c) title and DP remarks are clean.

## A better note (structure)
1. **Recommendation** (one line, with conditions).
2. **Deal:** parcel, price, structure.
3. **Three numbers:** margin, IRR, peak funding.
4. **Key assumptions** with sources: FSI table/road width, efficiency, price comps, absorption comps, TDR price.
5. **Sensitivities:** price −10%, absorption 20 quarters, TDR +35%, cost +10%.
6. **Top risks and mitigations:** title, approvals, market.
7. **Next steps and decision needed:** token amount, diligence timeline, walk-away price.

## What a strong candidate says that a weak one misses
- **Finds the errors in order of impact**, starting with area (gross vs net, SBU vs carpet, no ancillary) and TDR, then costs.
- **Rebuilds the numbers** rather than just criticising.
- **Points out the missing time dimension:** a feasibility without IRR and peak funding can't be compared with other uses of capital.
- **Calls out process risk:** pressure to sign this week is a negotiating tactic. Diligence timelines don't compress because the landowner wants money.
- **Critiques tone and evidence:** "55%!" should itself have been a warning. Pune fringe residential margins are rarely above 25–30%.
- **Is constructive:** gives the colleague a template, not just a list of faults.
