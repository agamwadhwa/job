# Case 02: outright vs JDA on a Hinjawadi plot

## Prompt
A 3-acre plot in Hinjawadi Phase 2, on a 24 m road. The owner offers two options: **outright at ₹15 cr/acre (₹45 cr)**, or a **JDA at 20% of gross revenue**. Your firm has ₹120 cr of equity available this year across all projects. Which option, and why?

**Assumptions:**
- Sale rate ₹9,000/sq ft carpet. Construction ₹2,500/sq ft of construction area.
- TDR ₹2,400/sq ft. ASR ₹12,000/sq m.
- 14 quarters of construction, 14 quarters of sales.
- Other parameters as REF-W.

## 5-minute framework answer
1. **Area:** net 11,534 sq m × 2.75 FSI × 1.6 = 5.46 lakh sq ft FSI area → **4.26 lakh sq ft carpet** → revenue ₹383 cr.
2. **Outright:** profit ₹76 cr pre-finance (19.7%), IRR 19.4%, **peak funding ₹118 cr**, nearly all of this year's equity.
3. **JDA at 20%:** profit ₹47 cr, IRR 20.6%, peak ₹78 cr. The break-even share is **18.2%**, so 20% costs slightly more than buying.
4. **Decision driver:** capital. With ₹120 cr for the whole year, outright blocks everything else. The JDA frees about ₹40 cr for another project.
5. **Recommendation:** JDA, negotiated to **≤ 18%**. If the owner insists on 20%, it's still acceptable only if the freed ₹40 cr has a use that earns more than about 20%.

## Full numbers

**Area statement:**
- Gross 12,141 sq m. Less 5% for road widening = 11,534 sq m.
- FSI 1.1 + 0.5 + 1.15 = 2.75 → 31,718 sq m. × 1.6 ancillary = 50,749 sq m = **5,46,261 sq ft**.
- Carpet (78%) **4,26,084 sq ft**. Construction area (1.3×) **7,10,140 sq ft**.

**Revenue:** 4,26,084 × 9,000 = **₹383.5 cr**.

**Costs (₹ cr, outright):**
- Land + stamp 48.2
- Approvals at Q0: 3.0
- Approvals, premium and ancillary at Q2: 10.9
- TDR: 1,42,772 sq ft × ₹2,400 = 34.3
- Construction + fees: 7,10,140 × 2,500 × 1.04 = 184.6
- Marketing + admin: 7% of revenue = 26.8
- Pre-finance profit **₹75.6 cr (19.7%)**. Unlevered IRR **19.4%**. Peak funding **₹117.6 cr**.

**JDA (developer's view, no land cost):**

| Landowner share | Landowner PV at 14% | Developer profit | Developer IRR | Developer peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15% | ₹39.6 cr | ₹66.3 cr | 28.6% | ₹75.4 cr |
| 18% | ₹47.5 cr | ₹54.8 cr | 23.8% | ₹76.5 cr |
| 20% | ₹52.8 cr | ₹47.1 cr | 20.6% | ₹77.6 cr |
| 25% | ₹66.0 cr | ₹27.9 cr | 12.4% | ₹80.5 cr |
| Outright | ₹48.2 cr (today) | ₹75.6 cr | 19.4% | ₹117.6 cr |

- **Break-even share** = 48.15 ÷ PV(collections) = **18.2%**.
- **Return on peak capital:** outright 75.6/117.6 = 0.64×. JDA at 20%: 47.1/77.6 = 0.61×. JDA at 18%: 0.72×.

**Capital lens:** outright uses ₹118 cr of the ₹120 cr budget. The JDA uses ₹78 cr, leaving ₹42 cr. If that ₹42 cr can fund a second project's peak (REF-W needs ₹41–55 cr), the JDA doubles the pipeline.

## What a strong candidate says that a weak one misses
- **Asks about the capital constraint first.** Without it, outright looks better on absolute profit, and that's the wrong answer for most growing developers.
- **Compares IRR, profit and peak funding together**, and computes the break-even share.
- **Notes the risk transfer:** in the JDA, a 10% price fall is partly borne by the landowner (20% of the revenue lost is his). In the outright case, the developer bears all of it.
- **Flags Hinjawadi-specific diligence:** MIDC vs PMRDA jurisdiction (Hinjawadi Phase 2 has both MIDC IT park and non-MIDC land: **verify** which authority applies), metro Line 3 timing (Hinjawadi–Shivajinagar: **verify status**), and traffic/access perception.
- **Checks the 24 m road** is existing and open, not just proposed. That's worth 0.25 FSI.
- **Structures the JDA:** landowner pays nothing, developer controls pricing, escrow for the landowner's share, and title CPs before any deposit.
- A weak candidate recommends outright "because profit is higher" or JDA "because it's cheaper". Neither is right without the capital lens.
