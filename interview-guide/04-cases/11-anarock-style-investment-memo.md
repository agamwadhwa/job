# Case 11: an ANAROCK-style investment memo

## Prompt
You're an analyst on an IPC capital-markets desk (ANAROCK/C&W/JLL style). A mid-size Pune developer wants **₹50 cr of structured debt (NCDs)** to part-fund land and approvals for the Hinjawadi project in Case 02 (outright option). The debt is at **17% IRR**, accruing, repaid as a bullet at quarter 10, secured on the land. Write the one-page investment memo you'd send to a credit fund, and give your recommendation on structure.

Use Case 02's outright cash flows: revenue ₹383.5 cr, pre-finance profit ₹75.6 cr, IRR 19.4%, peak funding ₹117.6 cr at quarter 5–6.

## 5-minute framework answer
1. **The ask fits the need:** land + stamp ₹48.2 cr at quarter 0. ₹50 cr of NCDs funds land and early approvals. The developer's equity funds TDR and construction until CF/collections.
2. **Repayment problem:** a bullet at Q10 = 50 × 1.17^2.5 ≈ **₹74 cr**, but cumulative project cash flow at Q10 is still **−₹82.6 cr**. The project can't repay from its own cash at Q10. It needs a **bank CF refinance** (post-approval) or a **collection sweep** instead of a bullet.
3. **Downside** (price −10%, sell-out 20 quarters): project IRR **7.4%**. Collections by Q10 fall from ₹135 cr to ₹85 cr.
4. **Recommendation:** proceed, restructured. Replace the bullet with a sweep of 25–30% of collections from launch (Q3), a refinance CP once CC and RERA are in, and security of land mortgage + share pledge + promoter guarantee. Covenants: minimum sales/price, no other debt.
5. **Pricing:** 17% is reasonable for land-stage risk with a listed-quality title. Consider 16% + a small upside kicker if the promoter wants a lower coupon.

## Full numbers

**Project cumulative cash flow (₹ cr, unlevered, outright), by quarter:**
Q0 −51.2 · Q1 −51.2 · Q2 −62.1 · Q3 −107.0 · Q4 −114.0 · Q5 −117.6 · Q6 −117.7 · Q7 −114.2 · Q8 −107.2 · Q9 −96.6 · **Q10 −82.6** · Q11 −65.0 · Q12 −43.9 · Q13 −19.3 · Q14 +8.8 · Q15 +40.5 · Q16 +75.6

**Bullet at Q10:** 50 × 1.17^(10/4) = **₹74.0 cr** → must be refinanced. The project doesn't generate positive cumulative cash until Q14.

**Collections by Q10:** base ₹134.6 cr. Downside ₹84.8 cr.

**A sweep alternative:** 30% of collections from Q3 to Q10 = 30% × 134.6 ≈ ₹40 cr of repayment. The balance (~₹34 cr) is refinanced by bank CF, or swept over Q11–Q13. In the downside, the sweep repays ≈ ₹25 cr by Q10. The fund relies more on refinance and security.

**Security cover at Q0:** land value ₹45 cr (at cost) ÷ ₹50 cr = **0.9×**. Weak alone. Add a pledge of the SPV shares and a promoter personal/corporate guarantee. After approvals, land + approvals value rises (the residual value of an approved parcel is higher). Cover improves to about 1.3–1.5× (assumption).

## The one-page memo (format to reproduce in an interview)

> **Investment memo: ₹50 cr structured debt, [Developer] Hinjawadi Phase 2 residential**
>
> **Ask:** ₹50 cr senior secured NCDs, 17% IRR, 3.5-year tenor, for land acquisition (₹48 cr incl. stamp) and early approvals.
>
> **Project:** 3 acres, 24 m road, 4.26 lakh sq ft carpet, GDV ₹384 cr at ₹9,000/sq ft. Project IRR 19.4% (unlevered), margin 19.7%, peak funding ₹118 cr (Q5–6).
>
> **Why it works:** Hinjawadi Phase 2 demand from the IT employment base; land at ~₹1,130 per carpet sq ft (12.6% of revenue); clean approvals path under PMRDA/MIDC (**to verify**).
>
> **Key risks:** (1) repayment depends on refinance, as the project is cash-negative until Q14; (2) absorption, since a 20-quarter sell-out plus 10% price cut takes project IRR to 7.4%; (3) approval timing; (4) promoter group leverage (**to diligence**).
>
> **Structure proposed:** land mortgage + SPV share pledge + PG; escrow of all collections; sweep 30% from launch; refinance CP within 6 months of CC; covenants on min sales (units/quarter) and min price (₹8,500); no additional debt at SPV; step-in rights.
>
> **Recommendation:** proceed to term sheet, subject to title, approvals timeline and group leverage diligence.

## What a strong candidate says that a weak one misses
- **Tests the repayment source against the cash-flow timing.** The bullet doesn't work, and a weak candidate misses it.
- **Runs a downside** and states how the structure protects the lender in it.
- **Sizes security honestly:** land alone at 0.9× cover is insufficient at entry.
- **Knows the regulatory reason** the developer needs this money: banks don't fund land.
- **Keeps the memo to one page** with numbers first.
- **Flags diligence items** instead of asserting them (group leverage, the approvals authority).
