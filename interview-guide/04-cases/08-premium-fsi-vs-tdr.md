# Case 08: a premium FSI vs TDR decision

## Prompt
On REF-W (2 acres, Wagholi, 18 m road), plans are being finalised. The TDR broker quotes ₹2,200/sq ft today, but says prices are "going up 20% in the next year". The promoter asks three questions:
1. Should we load the full 0.9 TDR at all, or build only basic + premium?
2. If we load it, buy all now or in two lots?
3. At what TDR price does loading stop making sense?

## 5-minute framework answer
1. **Marginal analysis per sq m of TDR:** 1 sq m of TDR plus its 60% ancillary gives 1.6 sq m of FSI area = 13.4 sq ft of carpet → ₹1.01 lakh of revenue. Costs: construction ₹53.6k, TDR ₹23.7k, statutory + ancillary ₹3.1k, marketing ₹7.1k → **₹13.3k margin per sq m**. Load it.
2. **Premium FSI is much cheaper per sq m** (₹4,500 vs ₹23,700 for TDR). Always take premium first. It's usually a given.
3. **Break-even TDR price ≈ ₹3,440/sq ft.** Above that, loading TDR destroys value.
4. **Timing:** buying all now (₹16.4 cr) vs half now, half in a year at +20% (PV ₹16.8 cr at 14%) → **buy now** if the rise is credible. But check that approvals and the phasing let you use it. TDR has to be loaded against a building permission.
5. **Also:** confirm the TDR's source and validity (the DRC, or development rights certificate), and check utilisation rules (e.g. slum TDR/"compulsory" components: **verify current UDCPR TDR utilisation rules**).

## Full numbers

**Per 1 sq m of TDR loaded:**

| | Amount (₹) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| FSI area created | 1.6 sq m = 17.22 sq ft | TDR + 60% ancillary |
| Carpet (78%) | 13.43 sq ft | |
| Revenue at ₹7,500 | 1,00,750 | |
| Construction | 53,554 | 17.22 × 1.3 × 2,300 × 1.04 |
| TDR cost | 23,681 | 10.764 sq ft × ₹2,200 |
| Ancillary premium | 540 | 0.6 × 10% × ₹9,000 |
| Other statutory | 2,583 | ₹150 × 17.22 |
| Marketing + admin (7%) | 7,053 | |
| **Margin** | **13,339** | 13.2% of revenue |

**Scaled:** the full 6,930 sq m of TDR → margin ≈ **₹9.2 cr**. Without TDR, the project loses 36% of its FSI area (FSI factor 2.5 → 1.6). Revenue falls about ₹70 cr, and fixed costs (land, approvals) are spread over less area.

**Break-even TDR price** (margin = 0): **₹3,439/sq ft**. At ₹2,640 (+20%), margin per sq m is ₹8,603. At ₹3,000, ₹4,728.

**Premium FSI per sq m:** 50% × ASR ₹9,000 = ₹4,500, about 1/5 the cost of TDR. Always taken first.

**Timing (all 74,594 sq ft of TDR):**
- All now: ₹16.41 cr
- Half now, half after one year at ₹2,640: 8.21 + 9.85/1.14 = **₹16.84 cr PV**. Buying now saves ₹0.43 cr in PV **if** the 20% rise happens.
- If prices stay flat: half now, half later = 8.21 + 8.21/1.14 = ₹15.41 cr PV. Deferring saves ₹1.0 cr.
- **Decision rule:** buy now only if P(rise ≥ 20%) is well above 50%, or supply is scarce. Otherwise stage purchases with approvals. A broker's forecast isn't evidence. Check recent DRC trade prices and supply from road/reservation surrenders.

## What a strong candidate says that a weak one misses
- **Thinks at the margin:** the question isn't "is the project profitable?" but "does the last sq m of TDR add value?".
- **Includes ancillary FSI on TDR** (1 sq m of TDR → 1.6 sq m of FSI area). Weak candidates forget it and undervalue TDR.
- **Computes a break-even price** and compares it with the market.
- **Treats the broker's forecast with scepticism**, and quantifies both rise and flat scenarios.
- **Checks TDR mechanics:** DRC validity, utilisation limits by road width, and any mandated share of specific TDR types (**verify current rules**). TDR can only be loaded with plan approval.
- **Links to design:** can the building actually use the extra FSI (height, fire rules for higher floors, setbacks)?
- **Mentions liquidity:** TDR tied up early raises peak funding (REF-W buys it at Q3, the jump from ₹24.8 cr to ₹48.1 cr cumulative outflow).
