# Case 05: a construction finance ask on a stalled project

## Prompt
You're an associate at an NBFC/credit fund. A Pune developer has a 400-unit project in Undri (**illustrative**) that's been stalled for 9 months after a cost overrun. Status:
- 220 units sold (55%), receivables from sold units ₹45 cr.
- Construction 45% complete. Remaining cost to complete ₹80 cr.
- Existing bank CF outstanding ₹40 cr, now overdue.
- 180 unsold units, average 800 sq ft carpet, current market ₹6,500/sq ft.

The developer asks you for **₹60 cr**. Do you lend, how much, and on what terms?

## 5-minute framework answer
1. **Can the project finish on its own?** Receivables ₹45 cr against ₹80 cr to complete: cover **0.56×**. No.
2. **With unsold inventory:** 1.44 lakh sq ft × ₹6,500 = ₹93.6 cr. With a 25% haircut ₹70.2 cr. Total cover (45 + 70.2)/80 = **1.44×**. The project is viable to complete.
3. **The funding gap is ₹35 cr, not ₹60 cr.** The rest of the ask is probably to repay the overdue bank or other group needs. Don't fund that.
4. **Structure:** ~₹40 cr last-mile (gap + ₹5 cr contingency), with **priority over the existing lender** (an intercreditor agreement or standstill), escrow on all collections, disbursement against LIE certificates, and a sweep from unsold sales.
5. **Conditions:** existing lender's consent and standstill, RERA extension, homebuyer communication, an independent cost audit, and a promoter guarantee. Price: 16–18% (assumption).

## Full numbers

**Sources and uses to completion (₹ cr):**
- Uses: cost to complete 80.0.
- Sources: receivables from sold units (collected as construction progresses) 45.0, plus **last-mile funding 35.0 (+5 contingency = 40)**.
- **After completion:** unsold inventory realises ₹70–94 cr (haircut to full value). That repays last-mile ₹40 cr + interest (~₹8–10 cr over 18–24 months), then the existing bank ₹40 cr + overdue interest.

**Cover on combined debt** (existing 40 + new 40 = 80) from receivables + haircut unsold: (45 + 70.2)/80 = **1.44×**. At the ₹60 cr ask, combined debt is ₹100 cr → cover **1.15×**. Too thin.

**Priority matters:** without priority, the new money ranks alongside or behind ₹40 cr of overdue bank debt. With priority on escrow flows, the new lender is repaid first from unsold sales.

**Stress:** if unsold units sell at ₹5,500 (−15%) with the same haircut: 1,44,000 × 5,500 × 0.75 = ₹59.4 cr. Last-mile ₹40 cr + interest ₹9 cr = ₹49 cr is still covered (1.2×). The existing bank takes the hit.

## What a strong candidate says that a weak one misses
- **Separates the funding gap from the ask** and refuses to fund anything outside the project.
- **Diagnoses why it stalled:** a cost overrun from what? (Design change, contractor failure, fund diversion?) Asks for an independent QS/LIE cost audit. My contracting background is relevant here: check whether RA bills matched work done, and whether materials were paid for but not delivered.
- **Checks RERA:** is the registration valid or extended? Were 70% account withdrawals certified properly? If money was diverted, that's a red flag for the promoter.
- **Intercreditor dynamics:** the existing bank must agree to subordinate or stand still. Otherwise the new lender's security is contested. In insolvency, homebuyers are financial creditors under the IBC, which complicates recovery.
- **Homebuyer communication:** a stalled project's buyers may have stopped paying. Receivables of ₹45 cr assume they resume. Verify with a sample of buyer confirmations.
- **Knows the SWAMIH-type precedent** for last-mile funding of stalled housing (check current fund status).
- A weak candidate lends ₹60 cr because "cover is 1.44×", forgetting that ₹20 cr leaks out of the project and that cover must be on total debt.
