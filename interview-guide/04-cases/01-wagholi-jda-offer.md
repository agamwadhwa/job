# Case 01: a JDA offer on 2 acres in Wagholi

## Prompt
A landowner in Wagholi offers his 2-acre plot (on an 18 m road, residential zone, clean 7/12) to your firm on a JDA: **40% area share** to the landowner. He also wants ₹3 cr as a refundable deposit on signing. Your promoter asks: "Do we take it? If not, what do we counter with?"

Use REF-W for the project (see `00-how-to-use.md`). The land's outright market value is about ₹8 cr/acre.

## 5-minute framework answer
1. **What does the plot yield?** 2.59 lakh sq ft of carpet (REF-W), revenue ₹194 cr at ₹7,500/sq ft.
2. **What is 40% worth to him?** About 1.03 lakh sq ft, roughly **₹78 cr**, against an outright value of ₹16 cr.
3. **What does it leave us?** We build 100% and sell 60%: a **loss of about ₹19 cr** before finance.
4. **What's the fair share?** Break-even revenue share ≈ **12.3%**. At 15% revenue share, our IRR matches outright (23.6%) with less capital.
5. **Recommendation:** decline 40% area. Counter with **13–15% revenue share (gross, excluding GST)**. Take the deposit only if it's refundable against title milestones. Walk away if he won't go below about 18%.

## Full numbers

**Project (REF-W):** carpet 2,58,592 sq ft. Revenue ₹193.9 cr. Costs excluding land: ₹145.8 cr (including finance ₹5 cr). Pre-finance surplus before land ₹53.1 cr.

**Option A: 40% area share (his offer).**
- Landowner's flats: 40% × 2,58,592 = 1,03,437 sq ft → worth ₹77.6 cr at ₹7,500.
- Developer sells 60% = 1,55,155 sq ft → revenue ₹116.4 cr.
- The developer still pays full construction, TDR, premiums and approvals (no land cost).
- Developer's pre-finance profit: **−₹19.0 cr**, IRR negative, peak funding ₹47.8 cr. **Unworkable.**

**Option B: revenue share (the counter).** The developer controls all sales. No land cost.

| Landowner share | Landowner nominal | Landowner PV at 14% | Developer profit (pre-finance) | Developer IRR | Developer peak funding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12% | ₹23.3 cr | ₹16.7 cr | ₹29.9 cr | 29.2% | ₹40.3 cr |
| 15% | ₹29.1 cr | ₹20.9 cr | ₹24.0 cr | 23.6% | ₹41.1 cr |
| 18% | ₹34.9 cr | ₹25.0 cr | ₹18.2 cr | 18.0% | ₹41.8 cr |
| Outright ₹16 cr | — | ₹17.1 cr (paid today) | ₹36.0 cr | 23.6% | ₹54.7 cr |

**Reading the table:**
- At **15%**, the developer earns the same IRR as buying outright and saves ₹13.6 cr of peak funding. The landowner gets PV ₹20.9 cr, about 22% more than selling outright.
- At **18%**, IRR falls to 18%, which is likely below the hurdle.
- **The deposit:** ₹3 cr refundable, adjusted against his share over the collection period. It costs about ₹0.7 cr in PV (by the method in `03-technical/03`, P5, scaled). Accept it if it's tied to milestones: title report, public notice with no objections, and plan sanction.

**Pitch to the landowner (in words he'll accept):** "At 40% of flats, the project loses money and no developer can finish it. At 15% of every rupee we collect, you get about ₹29 cr over four years, nearly double the ₹16 cr you'd get selling today. And you don't have to sell any flats yourself."

## What a strong candidate says that a weak one misses
- **Converts area share to rupees immediately** and compares with outright value. Weak candidates debate 40% vs 35% without numbers.
- **Explains why revenue share beats area share** for both sides: one price list, no competing seller, no fights over which flats.
- **Computes the break-even share** (12.3%) and **uses IRR and peak funding**, not just profit, to justify a counter above it (15%).
- **Separates gross vs net** revenue share, and excludes GST.
- **Prices the deposit** and ties it to title milestones.
- **Checks what's behind "clean 7/12"**: mutation history, heirs, and DP remarks for road widening.
- **Knows the walk-away point** before negotiating.
- Mentions that JDA headline shares vary by micro-market. Lodha's Wakad JDA at 22.5% of revenue ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/lodha-signs-development-agreement-for-20-468-sqm-plot-in-pune-s-wakad-125042400406_1.html)) reflects Wakad's higher land value relative to cost, not a Pune standard.
