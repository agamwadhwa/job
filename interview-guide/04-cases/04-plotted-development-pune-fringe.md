# Case 04: a plotted development on the Pune fringe

## Prompt
20 acres of agricultural land in a residential zone of the PMRDA plan, 35–40 km from central Pune, near a proposed ring-road alignment (**verify status**). Price ₹1.5 cr/acre. The promoter wants to do plotted development: sell NA plots with roads, water, electricity and a clubhouse. Is it worth doing, and what's the biggest risk?

**Assumptions:**
- 55% of gross area saleable (after roads, open space, amenity space).
- Plot rate ₹1,800/sq ft of plot area.
- Development cost ₹180/sq ft of gross area.
- Approvals (NA/conversion, layout sanction, RERA) ₹2.5 cr.
- Marketing + admin 6%. Stamp 7% on land.
- Buyers pay 20% on booking and 80% on registration of the plot, once the layout is developed (Q7).
- Sales over 12 quarters.

## 5-minute framework answer
1. **Saleable:** 20 acres × 43,560 × 55% = **4.79 lakh sq ft** of plots ≈ 320 plots of 1,500 sq ft. Revenue ₹86 cr.
2. **Costs:** land ₹32.1 cr (with stamp), development ₹15.7 cr, approvals ₹2.5 cr, marketing ₹5.2 cr → **profit ₹30.8 cr (36%)**, IRR **31%**, peak ₹42.6 cr.
3. **Why plotted:** low capex, no construction risk, fast cash at registration. The margin is high because the product is simple.
4. **The biggest risk is absorption, not cost.** Fringe plot demand is speculative and depends on infrastructure. Sales over 16 quarters → IRR 25%. Over 8 quarters → 39%.
5. **Recommendation:** viable at these assumptions if the ring road's timing is credible. **Phase it** (launch 8 acres first), and verify the zone, access road and title across what may be many 7/12s.

## Full numbers

**Area:**
- Gross 8,71,200 sq ft. Saleable 55% = 4,79,160 sq ft.
- The 45% deduction covers internal roads (~25–30%), open space/recreational ground (10% under UDCPR for layouts: **verify**) and amenity space (**verify** % by layout size).

**Revenue:** 4,79,160 × 1,800 = **₹86.2 cr**.

**Costs (₹ cr):** land 20 × 1.5 × 1.07 = 32.1. Development 8,71,200 × 180 = 15.7. Approvals 2.5. Marketing + admin 6% = 5.2. **Total 55.4. Profit ₹30.8 cr (35.7%).**

**Cash flow (quarterly):**
- Q0: land + part approvals.
- Q1: balance approvals.
- Q2–Q7: development.
- From Q2: sales, 20% on booking.
- Q7: 80% on registration for plots sold to date. Plots sold after Q7 pay the full amount on booking/registration.

**Results:** unlevered IRR **30.9%**, peak funding **₹42.6 cr**.

**Sensitivity:**

| Case | Profit (₹ cr) | IRR | Peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base: ₹1,800, 12 quarters | 30.8 | 30.9% | 42.6 |
| 8-quarter sell-out | 30.8 | 39.2% | 40.1 |
| 16-quarter sell-out | 30.8 | 24.8% | 43.9 |
| Rate ₹2,200, 8 quarters (optimistic) | 48.8 | 59.4% | 38.4 |

Profit is unchanged by absorption in this model because it excludes holding costs. In reality, slower sales add marketing spend, maintenance of the layout, and price pressure.

**Compare apartments on the same land?** Apartments on the fringe need higher density demand and construction capital (4–5× the capex), and sell slower at 35–40 km out. Plotted suits speculative, infrastructure-led demand. Apartments suit end-user demand. Recommend plotted **unless** there's evidence of an employment hub nearby.

## What a strong candidate says that a weak one misses
- **Aggregation and title:** 20 acres on the fringe often means 5–15 survey numbers and many holders. Title risk dominates. Check tenancy/Class II history and gairan (government pasture) land.
- **Zone and layout rules:** confirm the residential zone in the PMRDA plan. Layout sanction needs roads, RG and amenity space. NA/conversion may be deemed in residential zones (**verify**). Plotted layouts need RERA registration.
- **Buyer financing:** banks lend less readily on plots than on apartments (plot loans usually need a construction commitment: **verify lender norms**). That slows sales and shifts buyers to cash investors, who are more cyclical.
- **Infrastructure dependence:** the ring-road premium is speculation until land acquisition and construction start. Phase launches to match.
- **Development quality** matters for reselling a plotted brand: roads, drainage, water source, electrification.
- **GST:** sale of plots is generally treated as sale of land (no GST), but development charges may attract it (**verify with tax**).
- A weak candidate sees a 36% margin and says yes, without asking about absorption, title or buyer financing.
