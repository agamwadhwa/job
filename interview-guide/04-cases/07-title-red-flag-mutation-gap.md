# Case 07: a title red flag (a 7/12 mutation gap)

## Prompt
You're in the land team. A 1.5-acre parcel near Kharadi is agreed at ₹12 cr (outright). A token of ₹25 lakh has been paid. The advocate's preliminary report says:
- The current 7/12 shows three brothers (A, B, C) as holders.
- **Mutation entry (ferfar) 2261 of 2004** records the inheritance from their father. But the father had **four** children. A sister (D) isn't recorded, and there's no release deed from her on file.
- **Mutation entry 2893 of 2011** records a sale of 0.2 acres from the same survey number to a third party, but the search report shows **no registered sale deed** for that year.
- The public notice drew one objection, from a person claiming an unregistered 2009 agreement to buy the land.

The promoter wants to close in 30 days. What do you do?

## 5-minute framework answer
1. **Three separate defects:**
   - (a) A **missing heir** (D). Her share may be 1/4 of the father's holding under the Hindu Succession Act (daughters have had equal coparcenary rights since the 2005 amendment. Applicability depends on facts and dates: **verify with the advocate**).
   - (b) A **mutation without a deed**: the 0.2-acre sale may be invalid, or the deed may be elsewhere. Either way the area and boundaries are uncertain.
   - (c) A **third-party claim** under an unregistered 2009 agreement.
2. **Don't close in 30 days.** Tell the promoter plainly, with the cost of closing anyway.
3. **Cures:**
   - (a) A registered **release deed from D**, with consideration paid directly to her, or D joins as a confirming party.
   - (b) Trace the 2011 deed (the Sub-Registrar index for adjacent years or the third party's copy), or carve out 0.2 acres and re-measure (TILR).
   - (c) Get the advocate's view on enforceability (unregistered, and possibly time-barred for specific performance: **verify**), then settle or get a written withdrawal.
4. **Restructure payment:** hold ₹3–4 cr in escrow until all three are cured. Revise the price if the area falls to 1.3 acres.
5. **Timeline:** 60–120 days, realistically. The cost of waiting is small compared with a clouded title.

## Full numbers

**Area impact if the 0.2-acre sale is valid:** 1.5 → 1.3 acres (−13%). At an agreed rate of ₹8 cr/acre, the price should fall **₹1.6 cr** to ₹10.4 cr.

**D's potential share:** if D is entitled to 1/4 of the father's holding, that's up to 0.375 acres of the 1.5 (or 0.325 of 1.3). A release is typically negotiated at market value of her share, ≈ ₹2.6–3.0 cr at ₹8 cr/acre, paid out of the ₹12 cr price, not on top. The brothers pay her.

**Cost of delay (60–90 days):**
- Carry on the token only: trivial.
- Opportunity cost of project start: on a REF-W-type project, a 2-quarter delay costs about 2 IRR points (23.6% → 21.6%).
- **Cost of closing anyway:** if D sues after launch, sales and lender disbursements may freeze. Months of delay on a live project with buyers under RERA. The expected cost is far above a 90-day wait. (Illustrative: a 10% chance of a 12-month freeze on a ₹190 cr revenue project, where each quarter's delay costs ~₹1–2 cr of carry plus RERA interest to buyers, is an expected ₹0.5–0.8 cr before reputational cost. The real problem is that it's unbounded.)

**Revised payment structure (₹ cr):**
- Token 0.25 (paid)
- On D's release deed + mutation correction + deed trace for the 2011 sale: 4.0
- On withdrawal/settlement of the 2009 claimant + clean public notice: 3.0
- On registration of conveyance: balance (adjusted for final area)
- Escrow retention 1.0 for 6 months post-registration (mutation in the buyer's name)

## What a strong candidate says that a weak one misses
- **Reads the mutation history**, not just the current 7/12, and names each defect separately.
- **Knows the order of cures** and who pays: the sellers cure their own title. The buyer doesn't pay D on top.
- **Uses escrow and conditions precedent**, not trust.
- **Tells the promoter "no" to 30 days** with numbers, and offers a path to "yes".
- **Doesn't give a legal conclusion** on D's share or the 2009 claim. Asks the advocate, but knows the right questions.
- **Measures the land** (TILR) before finalising the area-based price.
- A weak candidate says "get an indemnity from the brothers and close". An indemnity from the parties whose title is defective is weak security.
