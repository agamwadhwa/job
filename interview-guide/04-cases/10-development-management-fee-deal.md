# Case 10: a development management (DM) fee deal

## Prompt
A manufacturing company owns 5 acres of surplus land in Kharadi (residential zone after conversion, on a 24 m road). It has cash and a low cost of capital. It's choosing between:
- **A. DM:** the company funds and owns the project. Your firm designs, gets approvals, builds (as manager) and sells for a **fee of 10% of revenue**, with your brand giving a 5% price premium.
- **B. JDA:** your firm funds everything. The company gets **30% of gross revenue**.
- **C. Outright sale** to the highest bidder.

You're on the developer's side. Which structure do you pitch, and how do you explain the trade-off to the company's CFO?

**Assumptions:**
- Net plot 19,223 sq m. FSI 2.75 + 60% ancillary → 9.10 lakh sq ft FSI area → **7.10 lakh sq ft carpet**.
- Base rate ₹11,000 (₹11,550 with the brand premium). Construction ₹2,600/sq ft on 11.84 lakh sq ft.
- TDR ₹2,400/sq ft. ASR ₹15,000/sq m. 14 quarters of construction and sales.
- Marketing + admin 7%. Discount rates: company 10%, developer 14%.

## 5-minute framework answer
1. **Project size:** revenue ≈ **₹820 cr**, pre-land surplus about ₹360 cr before the fee or share.
2. **Company's value (PV):** DM ≈ **₹179 cr at 10%** (₹149 cr at 14%), but the company funds a ₹130 cr peak and carries all market risk. JDA at 30% ≈ **₹187 cr at 10%** (₹169 cr at 14%) with no funding or risk. Outright ≈ what a developer bidding at an 18–20% IRR hurdle would pay: **₹156–169 cr**.
3. **Developer's value:** DM fee ≈ ₹82 cr nominal (PV ₹56.5 cr) with no capital. JDA at 30%: profit ₹114 cr, IRR 28%, but ₹132 cr of peak capital.
4. **Pitch:** if the company wants maximum value with no risk, the JDA at about 30% beats both DM (after risk) and outright. If the company insists on control and upside, DM, and the developer earns a capital-free fee.
5. **Developer's preference:** JDA if capital is available (bigger absolute profit, IRR 28%). DM if capital is scarce (a fee with no capital, near-infinite ROE).

## Full numbers

**Area:**
- Net 5 × 4,047 × 0.95 = 19,223 sq m. × 2.75 × 1.6 = 84,582 sq m = **9,10,435 sq ft** FSI area.
- Carpet 7,10,140 sq ft. Construction 11,83,566 sq ft.
- TDR 19,223 × 1.15 × 10.764 = 2,37,944 sq ft × ₹2,400 = **₹57.1 cr**.
- Approvals, premium and ancillary ≈ ₹25.6 cr (₹3 cr upfront + ₹22.6 cr at plan approval).

**Revenue with brand premium:** 7,10,140 × 11,550 = **₹820.2 cr**.

**Option A: DM (company's view).**
- Company funds all costs, including the ₹82.0 cr fee (10%) and 7% marketing/admin.
- Company's nominal profit (land at zero cost) **₹278 cr**. IRR 60% (on land-free cash flows: meaningless without a land value). **Peak funding ₹130 cr.**
- NPV at 10% = **₹178.6 cr**. At 14% = ₹149.3 cr.

**Option B: JDA.**

| Company share | Company nominal | PV at 10% | PV at 14% | Developer profit | Developer IRR | Developer peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25% | ₹205 cr | — | ₹141 cr | ₹155 cr | 37.9% | ₹128 cr |
| 30% | ₹246 cr | ₹187 cr | ₹169 cr | ₹114 cr | 28.2% | ₹132 cr |
| 35% | ₹287 cr | — | ₹198 cr | ₹73 cr | 18.4% | ₹137 cr |

**Option C: outright.** The residual land value a developer would pay at an 18% IRR hurdle is **₹169 cr**. At 20%, **₹156 cr** (≈ ₹31–34 cr/acre). A competitive process might reach the upper end.

**Explaining it to the CFO:**
- "DM gives you the most nominal profit (₹278 cr), but you fund ₹130 cr at peak and bear the full price and sales risk. In PV at your 10% cost of capital, it's ₹179 cr, slightly *less* than the JDA's ₹187 cr, which you get with zero funding and shared risk."
- "Outright gives certainty today (₹156–169 cr), but you give up the upside."
- "The JDA sits in the middle: more value than selling, less risk than DM."
- **The DM fee in context:** 10% is the Godrej Properties–Godrej & Boyce Vikhroli benchmark ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/godrej-properties-signs-agreement-with-godrej-and-boyce/20351.html)). It pays for brand premium, design, approvals and sales. The 5% brand premium alone recovers half the fee (₹39 cr of ₹82 cr).

## What a strong candidate says that a weak one misses
- **Compares options in PV at the right discount rate for each party.** The company's 10% and the developer's 14% give different answers.
- **Values risk:** DM's higher nominal profit comes with funding and market risk. The JDA shifts both to the developer.
- **Explains the DM fee's economics:** what the fee pays for, and that the brand premium partly offsets it.
- **Governance in DM:** the landowner-funder must commit funding (escrowed), or the developer's brand is at risk from underfunding. Include step-in and termination clauses.
- **Checks the change of use:** surplus industrial land converting to residential needs permission and may carry premiums or amenity obligations (**verify current UDCPR provisions**).
- A weak candidate compares nominal profits (DM ₹278 cr > JDA ₹246 cr) and recommends DM, ignoring funding, risk and time.
