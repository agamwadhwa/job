# Case 09: absorption slowdown sensitivity

## Prompt
REF-W was approved at IC on 12-quarter absorption and ₹7,500/sq ft. Six months after launch, bookings are running at **60% of plan**, and a competitor 500 m away has launched at ₹7,100. The promoter asks: "How bad is it, and what should we do?"

## 5-minute framework answer
1. **Translate to the model:** 60% of plan velocity ≈ 20-quarter sell-out instead of 12.
2. **Impact:** IRR 23.6% → **13.4%**. Peak funding ₹54.7 cr → **₹64.9 cr** (+₹10 cr of capital). Nominal profit is unchanged, but that ignores extra holding and marketing cost.
3. **Options:** (a) cut price to ₹7,125 (−5%) to restore velocity to about 16 quarters → IRR 13.2%, i.e. about the same. (b) Hold price, slow construction on the unsold towers (phase), and push sales channels. (c) Change the product/payment plan (a subvention-like plan or a smaller unit mix).
4. **The grid shows** price and velocity trade off roughly 1:1 in IRR terms here: −5% price ≈ +4 quarters. So a price cut is only worth it if it buys **more than** 4 quarters of velocity.
5. **Recommendation:** don't cut headline price yet. Phase construction (defer tower 3), add channel partners for 2 quarters, and re-test. Cut 5% only if velocity doesn't recover to 80% of plan.

## Full numbers

**IRR grid (unlevered, REF-W; rows = sell-out quarters, columns = price ₹/sq ft):**

| Quarters | ₹6,750 | ₹7,125 | ₹7,500 | ₹7,875 | Peak funding at ₹7,500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 16.9% | 24.8% | 32.4% | 39.8% | ₹49.7 cr |
| 12 (plan) | 12.2% | 18.0% | **23.6%** | 29.0% | ₹54.7 cr |
| 16 | 8.9% | 13.2% | 17.4% | 21.5% | ₹59.7 cr |
| 20 (current pace) | 6.8% | 10.1% | **13.4%** | 16.5% | ₹64.9 cr |
| 24 | 5.4% | 8.1% | 10.7% | 13.3% | ₹70.0 cr |

**Reading it:**
- From (20 quarters, ₹7,500) = 13.4%: a 5% cut to ₹7,125 must bring sell-out to about **16 quarters** just to match (13.2%). To reach 18% IRR at ₹7,125 you need a 12-quarter pace. That's full plan velocity at a lower price.
- Holding price and recovering to 16 quarters gives 17.4%.

**Hidden costs not in the grid:**
- Extra marketing and brokerage to push sales: say +1% of revenue ≈ ₹1.9 cr.
- Holding costs on unsold completed units (maintenance, property tax), and finance on the extra ₹10 cr of peak funding (at 12% over a year ≈ ₹1.2 cr).
- **Price-cut signalling:** existing buyers may demand parity, and brand value takes a hit.

**Phasing lever:** if tower 3 (say 30% of the area) is deferred by 4 quarters, construction spend is pushed out, so peak funding falls, and the unsold inventory isn't built ahead of demand. It doesn't fix IRR fully, but it caps capital at risk.

## What a strong candidate says that a weak one misses
- **Quantifies before recommending**, and uses the grid to show the price–velocity trade-off.
- **Diagnoses the cause:** is it price (the competitor at ₹7,100), product (unit sizes), channel (few brokers), or the market (Wagholi months of inventory, see `03-technical/04` P5)? The fix depends on it.
- **Separates list price from effective price:** offers such as stamp duty waivers, flexible payment plans or floor-rise waivers can move velocity without a headline cut (while complying with RERA and not misleading buyers).
- **Uses phasing** to protect capital.
- **Protects covenants:** if there's CF with minimum sales/price covenants, a price cut may breach the minimum price. Talk to the lender first.
- A weak candidate says "cut price 10%" (IRR falls to 12.2% even at plan pace) or "wait and see" without a trigger.
