## 10 likely questions
All typical for this role type (unverified for this firm).

### Q1 [cap] Senior debt, mezzanine, structured equity: explain the difference to a developer.
Answer pointer: seniority, security and return. Senior CF is secured on the project with escrow, 10–12%. Mezzanine is subordinated or second charge, sometimes on promoter shares, 15–20%. Structured equity carries an IRR hurdle with a promoter buyback, 18–24% (all assumptions).

### Q2 [cap] How would you structure ₹75 cr for a developer whose project is 40% sold but stuck on cost overrun?
Answer pointer: see case 04-cases/05. Check cost-to-complete vs receivables from sold units plus unsold inventory value. Last-mile funding with priority on escrow and a step-in right.

### Q3 [cap] What's a security cover ratio?
Answer pointer: (value of security or receivables) ÷ outstanding loan. Lenders want about 1.5–2.0× on receivables plus unsold inventory (assumption).

### Q4 [cf] What are "receivables" and "unsold inventory value" in a construction finance deal?
Answer pointer: receivables = the balance due from sold units. Unsold value = unsold area × current rate, often with a haircut.

### Q5 [cap] Why do Indian residential developers use NCDs instead of bank loans?
Answer pointer: banks are restricted on land funding, and NBFCs/AIFs can lend against land and approvals risk. NCDs let AIFs invest in a debt form with tax and structure flexibility.

### Q6 [feas] Model test: land ₹50 cr, construction ₹120 cr over 3 years, revenue ₹250 cr over 4 years. Rough IRR?
Answer pointer: profit ₹80 cr on peak funding of maybe ₹70–80 cr. Build the annual cash flow and solve. Practise the same shape in 03-technical/04.

### Q7 [cap] What's a waterfall?
Answer pointer: the order in which cash is distributed: return of capital, then preferred return, then GP catch-up, then carry split.

### Q8 [behav] Why a boutique over a bank or IPC?
Answer pointer: breadth of exposure on every deal. Be honest about wanting ownership.

### Q9 [land] What diligence red flags would kill a lending deal?
Answer pointer: title defects, litigation, a RERA non-compliance order, approval lapses, promoter guarantees already stretched, and diverted escrow.

### Q10 [cap] What's the difference between an AIF Category II and a Category III?
Answer pointer: Cat II covers PE/debt funds with no leverage beyond operational needs, which is where most RE funds sit. Cat III uses complex strategies and leverage (hedge funds). **Verify current** SEBI rules.
